Sacred geometry · morphogenesis rig

Growing animals out of φ

A harness for building creatures from closed-form mathematics. You supply one measured number and a short declarative spec; the rig supplies the skin, the pelt, the light and the console. Nine species so far, from a nine-centimetre frog to a twenty-five-metre whale. This is the manual for writing one — and the argument for writing it only once.

The idea

Don't sculpt — grow. An animal is not a mesh someone pushed into shape; it is a small number of proportions, repeated, with volume hung on them. If the proportions are right the silhouette is right, and if the silhouette is right the animal reads as itself from across a field.

So the rig admits exactly one measured number per species, its anchor. Everything else is that anchor times a power of φ, or a fraction of a length that is itself a power of φ. Skin is one continuous signed-distance field. Fur is real strands placed by a low-discrepancy sequence. Colour, hair length and guard-hair banding are read out of the Mandelbrot set. There is no noise function, no texture, and no random() anywhere in the project: the same input grows the identical animal, hair for hair.

The rig is species-agnostic. A spec is species-specific. Nothing in src/rig/ knows what a fox is.

The φ cascade

Choose the dimension the world already measures this animal by — withers height for a quadruped, wingspan for a bird, total length for a fish — then write the master lengths as powers of φ and pick the powers that land closest to the real animal. This is the thesis of the whole model. Get it right and the rest is bookkeeping; force it and nothing downstream will save you.

Vulpes vulpes, anchored at the withers

withersW40.0 cm · measured
head + bodyWφ64.7 cm
brushWφ/φ40.0 cm
headW/φ²15.3 cm
earW/φ³9.4 cm
elbowW/φ24.7 cm

Those powers were not fitted to the animal. The tail comes out equal to the shoulder height, the skull to 15 cm and the ear to 9 cm — which is a red fox, to the centimetre.

Equus ferus caballus, anchored the same way

withersW1.60 m · measured
body lengthW1.60 m
neckW/φ98.9 cm
headW/φ²61.1 cm
tailW/φ98.9 cm
earW/φ⁵14.4 cm

Body length equal to withers height is the classical "square" horse, and it falls out of the cascade rather than being imposed on it.

Anatomy of a spec

One file in src/species/, one default export. Every dimension in it is in anchor units — multiples of the anchor — so changing the anchor grows the whole animal without another edit.

FieldWhat it is
id, title, binomial, blurbIdentity, shown in the console.
anchorThe one measured number: key, label, value, range.
paramsExtra live sliders — leanness, coat, anything the spec reads from g.
derive(W)The φ cascade. Returns the master lengths plus a table for the console.
body(M, D, W, g)The implicit volume, written with the form vocabulary.
appendages(M, D, W, g)Lofted shells: ears, fins, frills.
coat(M, D, W, g)Palette, colour layers, hair-length layers, banding.
flowThe lay of the coat.
viewsFive camera directions. Distance is fitted automatically.
lattice, peltMarching-cubes cell size (anchor units) and strand count.

Form vocabulary

The spec never touches the field engine. It calls these verbs on M, and they take anchor units throughout.

VerbBuilds
M.trunk(table, k, per, orient)The torso, from rows of [station, topline, underline, half-width, exponent]. Also defines the coat's belly line, so geometry and markings can never disagree. orient: {axis:'y'} stands the trunk upright for a biped, belly forward — the man's table runs [height, front, back, half-width].
M.chain(stations, weld, steps)Any other swept run — neck, muzzle, jaw. Catmull-Rom, so no joins show.
M.st(x, y, r1, r2, n)A station on the midline. M.stAt(point, …) for one off it.
M.blob(p, rx, ry, rz, weld)A soft mass: skull, haunch, cheek, jowl.
M.at(name, x, y, z)A named landmark the coat can find later.
M.frame(name, from, to)A local frame. Give the head one and write the face as (along, up, across).
M.limb(side, joints, segs, opts)A limb; registers its bones so onLimb() can stocking them.
M.goldenCurve(name, opts)An arc of r = a·ebθ with b = ln φ/(π/2): the radius of curvature multiplies by φ every quarter turn.
M.curve(name, points)A plain centreline — a mane crest, a dorsal ridge.
M.sweep(name, radiusFn)Gives a curve flesh.

The superellipse exponent

Every swept cross-section is a Gielis superellipse. The exponent is the most useful single number in the rig: 2.0 is a plain ellipse, 2.6–3.0 is the flat-sided, deep-chested section of a real ribcage, and below 2 the section goes pinched and reptilian.

The weld

Volumes are joined with a polynomial smooth-minimum, and the weld width is the last argument everywhere. Wide welds where flesh genuinely merges — a thigh into a flank. Narrow welds along a chain, or the fillet bulges at every shared cap and the animal comes out ringed like a caterpillar.

Mask vocabulary

A mask answers "where on the animal", as a pure function returning 0 to 1. Markings, hair length and bare skin are all written as masks, which is why a spec contains no per-pixel code. Radii are in anchor units, so a mask written for a fox reads the same on a horse ten times its size.

MaskSelects
ventral(up, down)Below the belly line the trunk table defined.
dorsal(t0, t1)Along the back; 0 at the belly, 1 at the topline.
station(x0, x1)A longitudinal band.
height(y0, y1)Absolute height above the ground.
onLimb(r0, r1)Near any registered limb bone.
onCurve(name, r0, r1)Near a named curve — a tail, a mane crest.
alongCurve(name, s0, s1)A span from a curve's root to its tip. This is how the fox gets its white tag.
inFrame(name, {a, b, c})A soft box in a local frame. c is |across|, so left and right are written once.
near(mark, r0, r1)Distance from a landmark.
lateral(c0, c1) · facing(dir)How far out to the side; which way the surface faces.
fractal(lo, hi)The Mandelbrot escape time at this point, thresholded.
all · any · not · except · scaleCombinators. except(a, b) is the one you reach for most.

Coat and pelt

Colour is a stack of layers: the first is the ground colour, and each later one is a palette entry painted through a mask. Hair length is the same idea with lengths instead of colours. A layer may set tip to override the colour at the end of each hair.

// the black stockings of a bay, and its black tail
{ colour: 'points', tip: 'points', mask: all(onLeg, height(0.40, 0.26)) },
{ colour: 'points', tip: 'points', mask: onCurve('tail', 0.16, 0.070) },

Banding

band says how strongly the Mandelbrot field bands each guard hair — pale root, dark subterminal band, light tip. High for a wild agouti coat like a fox; near zero for a horse, whose coat is barely banded at all.

Placement

Strands are placed by the R2 low-discrepancy sequence, the two-dimensional cousin of the golden ratio, so they land the way seeds land on a sunflower head: evenly, never on a grid, never in a clump. Triangles are weighted by hair length as well as area, so a brush or a mane comes out dense without being asked.

Flow

Fur combs backwards along the body by default, radiates from a landmark you name (a nose), runs down below a height you give, and follows any curve you list. A mane is a curve plus a long-hair layer — nothing more.

The one-shot rule

The rig exists so that a creature can be produced in a single pass. That is the whole point, and it is fragile: an hour of nudging numbers by eye destroys the property that made the result interesting, and usually makes the animal worse, because eye-tuning optimises the current camera angle rather than the anatomy.

one anchorExactly one number about the animal comes from the world.
φ or nothingIf a decimal is within a few percent of a power of φ, write the power.
anchor unitsNo metres anywhere in a spec.
landmark, not look"The brisket sits at elbow height" survives a change of anchor. "A bit deeper" does not.
no facesEyes, teeth and claws are the fastest route into the uncanny valley, and they are not what makes an animal read as itself.
fix bugs onlyA tail launching in the wrong direction is a bug. A silhouette you'd prefer slightly different is not.

The fox in this repository was written once, then tuned for an hour. The tuning fixed three real bugs — a signed-distance term that clamped to zero inside its slab, vertex colours being read as linear, and smooth-min fillets bulging at every shared cap — and made every aesthetic judgement worse. The horse was written once and stopped.

The prompt

Paste this into a fresh session, replace the creature, and take the first output. The full text lives in PROMPT.md.

You are writing one file: a species spec for the sacred-geometry rig
at src/species/<id>.js. The rig already exists. You do not modify it.

THE RULE: exactly one number about <CREATURE> may be measured from the
world — its anchor. Every other dimension is that anchor times a power
of φ, or a fraction of a length that is itself a power of φ. Write the
φ expression in the source, not the decimal. All dimensions are in
anchor units, never metres.

1. Choose the anchor — the dimension the world already measures this
   animal by.
2. Build the φ cascade in derive(W): master lengths as powers of φ,
   choosing the powers that land closest to the real animal.
3. Write body(M, D, W, g) using only: trunk, chain, st, stAt, blob, at,
   frame, limb, goldenCurve, curve, sweep.
4. appendages() for anything too thin for a lattice.
5. coat(): colour and hair-length layers, each a palette entry through a
   composed mask. Set band for how agouti the coat is.
6. flow: base direction, the landmark fur radiates from, the height it
   runs down below, the curves it combs along.
7. views: five directions; distances are fitted.

Avoid: metres, decimals where a power of φ is close, eyes or teeth on
the first pass, and any change justified by "it looked better".

Output the complete file. Nothing else.

Worked example — what makes a horse not a large fox

Three things, and they are all in the cascade and the flow, not in the detail:

 FoxHorse
Necka jointW/φ — a third of the animal
Legsunder half the withersmost of the withers
Visible haireverywheremane, forelock, tail
Tail launchstreams backhangs down
Banding0.30 — wild agouti0.16 — barely
 
// the barrel: a horse's chest is deep and slab-sided, and its croup
// stands nearly as high as its withers
M.trunk([
  [ 0.470, 0.905, 0.560, 0.108, 2.6],   // point of shoulder
  [ 0.340, 0.985, 0.505, 0.132, 2.8],   // withers
  [ 0.170, 0.958, 0.470, 0.156, 3.0],   // girth
  [-0.140, 0.918, 0.528, 0.126, 2.7],   // flank
  [-0.420, 0.948, 0.548, 0.156, 3.0],   // croup
]);

// the crest — the ridge the mane grows from. A mane is a curve plus a
// long-hair layer, and nothing else.
M.curve('crest', [ … ]);
const onCrest = all(onCurve('crest', 0.115, 0.045), dorsal(0.72, 0.98));
fur: [ { len: 0.150 + 0.130 * g.coat, mask: onCrest } ]

Nine scenarios

Each species was written in one pass and then rendered. What follows is what each one taught the rig — every entry that says "rig fix" changed src/rig/, so no later spec can hit the same thing.

Equus caballuswithers 1.60 ma long neck; mane and tail as curvesrig fix: views became directions with the distance fitted to the animal, so a new species frames itself

What the scenarios confirmed

  • Anchor units carried a factor of 280 in scale — nine centimetres to twenty-five metres — without a single change to the rig.
  • Every unusual body plan was reachable with the same nine verbs. Nothing needed a new primitive except feathers.
  • Four of the nine species found a genuine rig bug. All four fixes were in src/rig/, not in the spec that found them, which is the whole point of separating the two.

Where it breaks

Recorded honestly, because a manual that only lists successes is useless for deciding what to build next.

feathersThere is no feather primitive. A lofted shell is a cup — right for an ear or a fin, wrong for a wing. Both birds read correctly in silhouette and poorly close up. A proper vaned-feather primitive (a rachis with two swept vanes) is the single highest-value thing to add.
hard edgesEverything is welded with a smooth-minimum, so nothing has a crease. A tortoise's shell rim, a fish's operculum and a bird's bill line all want a sharp edge the rig cannot currently produce.
grooves are paintWhale pleats and scute sulci are colour, not geometry. At a distance this is invisible; in a close-up it is obvious.
one poseEvery animal stands or hangs in the pose its spec describes. There is no skeleton to re-pose, and adding one would break the property that the whole body is one continuous field.
coplanar surfacesTwo volumes whose surfaces nearly coincide give marching cubes a near-zero gradient, and the mesh tears. Keep a blob decisively inside or decisively outside its neighbour.

Running it

python3 -m http.server 8080
# http://localhost:8080/rig.html?species=<id>

# vulpes  equus  mammut  chelonia  rana
# balaena  salmo  psittacus  vultur

./build-rig.sh   # one self-contained file per species, into dist/ and docs/models/

Three.js is vendored, so nothing needs the network — the files in docs/models/ open straight off disk. To add a species: write src/species/<id>.js, add one line to the registry in src/rig/app.js and one to assemble-rig.mjs, and load ?species=<id>.
